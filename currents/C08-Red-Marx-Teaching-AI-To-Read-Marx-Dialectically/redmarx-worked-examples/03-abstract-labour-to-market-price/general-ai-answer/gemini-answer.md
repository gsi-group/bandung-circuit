# What is the chain of determination from Abstract Labour to Market Price?

In Marxist political economy, the progression from **Abstract Labour** to **Market Price** represents the movement from the most fundamental, qualitative level of economic reality (the substance of value) to its most concrete, quantitative surface manifestation in a capitalist market.

Here is the step-by-step chain of determination:

---

## 1. Abstract Labour $\rightarrow$ Value

* **Abstract Labour**: All commodity production involves specific, concrete tasks (e.g., tailoring, weaving). When stripped of these concrete useful forms, human energy expended in general is **abstract labour**.
* **Value**: Abstract labour is the *substance* of value. The *magnitude* of value is determined by the **Socially Necessary Labour Time (SNLT)**—the average amount of time required to produce a given commodity under normal conditions of production with average skill and intensity.

$$
\text{Value} = \text{Constant Capital } (c) + \text{Variable Capital } (v) + \text{Surplus Value } (s)
$$

---

## 2. Value $\rightarrow$ Exchange Value & The Money Form

* **Exchange Value**: Value cannot express itself in isolation; it must express itself relatively through exchange with another commodity.
* **Money Form**: As commodity exchange expands, a single universal equivalent emerges (traditionally gold, now fiat money). The exchange value expressed in money is **Value Price** (or simple price), where price directly reflects the SNLT embodied in the commodity.

$$
\text{Value Price} = \frac{\text{Value of Commodity}}{\text{Value of Money Unit}}
$$

---

## 3. Value Price $\rightarrow$ Price of Production

In a developed capitalist economy, commodities do not sell at their direct values due to differing **organic compositions of capital** ($c : v$) across industries.

* **Equalization of the Profit Rate**: Capital flows freely from industries with low profit rates to high profit rates until an **average rate of profit** ($\bar{r}$) is established across the economy:

$$
\bar{r} = \frac{\sum s}{\sum (c + v)}
$$

* **Price of Production**: Values are transformed into prices of production. The price of production accounts for the cost price ($k = c + v$) plus the average profit on the total capital invested:

$$
\text{Price of Production} = k + \bar{r}K
$$

Where $K$ is the total advanced capital. At this level, individual commodities sell above or below their values, but the **sum of all prices of production equals the sum of all values**, and total profit equals total surplus value.

---

## 4. Price of Production $\rightarrow$ Market Price

* **Market Value / Market Price of Production**: Within a single industry, individual producers have different productivity levels. Competition within the sector yields a single market value or market price of production based on average industry productivity.
* **Supply, Demand, and Market Price**: Real-world supply and demand fluctuations cause the actual **Market Price** to oscillate continually around the Price of Production (its gravitational center).

$$
\text{Market Price} = \text{Price of Production} \pm \Delta_{\text{Supply/Demand/Monetary Distortions}}
$$

---

## Summary of the Chain

$$
\text{Abstract Labour} \xrightarrow{\text{SNLT}} \text{Value} \xrightarrow{\text{Money Form}} \text{Value Price} \xrightarrow{\text{Capital Competition}} \text{Price of Production} \xrightarrow{\text{Supply/Demand}} \text{Market Price}
$$

* **Substance Level**: Abstract labour forms Value.
* **Distribution Level**: Competition among capitals transforms Values into Prices of Production.
* **Surface Level**: Market dynamics cause Market Prices to fluctuate around Prices of Production.
